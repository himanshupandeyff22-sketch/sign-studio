# Everyday ASL reference audit — 12 October 2026

Scope: 32 proposed 3D-LEX source glosses. All 32 ASLU/Lifeprint HTML reference pages below were successfully retrieved through the web tool on 12 October 2026. The first BATHROOM request failed; a repeat succeeded, and TOILET independently describes the same lexical form. No unresolved reference URL remains in this requested set.

These are primary instructional references authored/published by ASL University (William G. Vicars). The observations below come from the retrieved reference text. Images and embedded videos are linked by those pages, but this audit did **not** download media, inspect source GLBs, watch all reference performances, or approve an Aarav animation. Source gloss agreement alone does not establish a motion's sense, variant, correctness, or suitability in a sentence. No expert approval is recorded.

Reference availability is separate from reuse permission. No Lifeprint image, video, pose, animation, or text corpus was imported. Linking and a brief original summary do not establish a redistribution license for its media or for an unrelated dataset.

## Reachable references and review cues

All rows have language **ASL**, reference status **retrieved**, target-motion comparison **pending**, and human linguistic validation **pending**. The sense restrictions are conservative implementation recommendations informed by the references, rather than approvals of a particular source animation.

| Source gloss | Retrieved primary reference | Direct reference observations and implications for review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bathroom | [ASLU BATHROOM](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/b/bathroom.htm) | Dominant T hand, palm outward; sideways shake or twist. A bathroom question can rely on raised brows and head position. ASLU rejects shaking B as a standard bathroom sign and recognizes R-hand RESTROOM separately. Do not manufacture BATH+ROOM. |
| toilet | [ASLU TOILET](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/toilet.htm) | Describes the same T-hand form as BATHROOM and notes bathroom as the usual interpretation. English room/use-bathroom versus porcelain-fixture senses must still be distinguished. Merge aliases only for a reviewed shared sense; do not import identical motion twice or silently merge fixture meaning. |
| sleep | [ASLU SLEEP](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/s/sleep.htm) | Open hand descends in front of the face while fingers gather toward the thumb, ending in a flattened O rather than S/fist. No necessary chin contact. SLEEP-IN and SLEEPY are linked separately; compare finger closure and face clearance. |
| tired | [ASLU TIRED](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/tired.htm) | Hands begin near HAVE and change orientation/slump rather than slide down. Exaggeration conveys exhaustion. Check both wrists, shoulders/body behavior and intensity; do not approve EXHAUSTED as an unconditional synonym. |
| doctor | [ASLU DOCTOR](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/d/doctor.htm) | Medical doctor has bent-hand, M and D variants tapping the base wrist. Academic doctor/title is expressly different. Record the actual variant; do not map professor or PhD to this lexical motion. Check dominant fingertip contact and base-hand orientation. |
| hospital | [ASLU HOSPITAL](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/h/hospital.htm) | H hand traces a cross on the shoulder; medical PATIENT is a separate linked entry. Check location, H fingers and cross movement after retargeting. Do not collapse hospital, doctor, patient and clinic into one concept. |
| hurt | [ASLU HURT/PAIN](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/h/hurt.htm) | Two extended index fingers approach with repeated jab or opposed twisting versions. Body location and force can express pain site/severity. A neutral isolated clip cannot encode every toothache/headache or emotional hurt sentence; confirm actual sense, variant and nonmanual expression. |
| hot | [ASLU HOT](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/h/hot.htm) | Claw hand leaves the mouth outward/downward; speed/intensity changes degree. Lexical temperature includes more than food, but the page explicitly distinguishes spicy and attractive-person meanings. Review the actual handshape, mouth location and intensity, keeping those English senses separate. |
| cold | [ASLU COLD](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/c/cold.htm) | Temperature sense uses shivering arms; not head-cold/illness. Stronger facial/body behavior and tighter faster movement express very cold. WINTER requires context; refrigerator and cool are separately discussed. Do not alias illness or winter blindly. |
| coffee | [ASLU COFFEE](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/c/coffee.htm) | Two-handed grinder-like movement; lower hand stays still while upper hand rotates. Drink subtypes and local invented initialized forms are discussed separately. Check two-hand coordination and do not extend one unknown COFFEE clip to latte, mocha or espresso. |
| tea | [ASLU TEA](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/tea.htm) | Dominant F with non-dominant C or O. Compare pinch shape, base-hand cup orientation and the complete movement against the linked demonstration; the text alone does not specify every motion detail. Keep beverage sense distinct from a meal/social tea context. |
| milk | [ASLU MILK](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/m/milk.htm) | C closes into S twice. Check genuine finger closure, repetitions and wrist stability. Sentence examples demonstrate that English conjunctions need not map directly into ASL; drink/noun reference is not approval of the verb milk or a product subtype. |
| apple | [ASLU APPLE](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/a/apple.htm) | Index knuckle at cheek with pivoting hand; page recommends X and warns about A-hand alternative. Require index articulation and cheek location. Fruit meaning does not license the Apple company/name sense. Record rather than silently normalize a source variant. |
| bread | [ASLU BREAD](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/b/bread.htm) | Dominant fingertips repeatedly slice along the base hand; pinkie-edge alternative is documented. Check base orientation/contact and variant. Food meaning only; English money/slang bread and product-specific bread forms are not established by the gloss. |
| fish | [ASLU FISH](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/f/fish.htm) | Animal form has a swimming-like moving hand, with another variant linked. Fishing/casting is explicitly different; repeated casting means FISHING. Do not automatically inflect FISH to FISHING as if it preserved the same sign. Animal/food context still needs mapping review. |
| vegetable | [ASLU VEGETABLE](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/v/vegetable.htm) | Cheek V twist versus index/middle alternating flip are documented variants; V-E-G spelling also exists. Lower location can change to VINEGAR. Check cheek location and variant; vegetarian and individual vegetable names require their own contextual review, not unrestricted aliases. |
| teacher | [ASLU TEACH/TEACHER](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/teacher.htm) | TEACHER combines TEACH with PERSON; formal and lower casual realization are documented. TEACH alone has movement/aspect variation. Inspect the whole compound, flat PERSON hands and bilateral motion; do not alias teach, teaching and teacher to one unchecked clip. |
| student | [ASLU STUDENT](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/s/student.htm) | LEARN plus PERSON; base hand remains near torso while dominant hand moves toward head. Formal/casual forms differ. Check both components and stationary base; avoid teacher-like bilateral head movement. Learner is a candidate shared sense, not automatic approval. |
| read | [ASLU READ](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/r/read.htm) | Dominant index/middle tips move over non-dominant flat page. Text describes single READ versus repeated READING. Preserve aspect distinctions for review; do not claim tense/ongoing activity from English inflection alone or map mind-reading automatically. |
| write | [ASLU WRITE](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/w/write.htm) | Pinched index/thumb and scribbling over stationary base palm. Downward, repeated and careful sideward versions depend on use. Check contact, shape and direction; type on a keyboard and author/create need separate meaning evidence. |
| book | [ASLU BOOK](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/b/book.htm) | Flat hands; repeated opening marks BOOK, one opening OPEN-BOOK, reversing CLOSE-BOOK. The text allows casual variation. Check both palms and repetition; reservation/to book is a different English sense and must not be an alias. |
| hearing | [ASLU HEARING](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/h/hearing.htm) | Cultural/person HEARING uses mouth-area small rotation; physical hearing uses ear taps. The page also discusses speak/say context. Source gloss alone cannot select these. Keep cultural/community/person, auditory ability and legal hearing meanings separate pending review. |
| again | [ASLU AGAIN](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/a/again.htm) | Bent dominant hand arcs fingertips into flat base palm in neutral space. Repeat/do over is a referenced shared sense. Check bend, arc and contact; sentence position/nonmanual question behavior still needs review. |
| today | [ASLU TODAY](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/today.htm) | NOW+NOW means TODAY or current time depending on context; NOW+DAY and reversed DAY versions are documented. Identify the exact realization rather than approve now/today as universal synonyms. Whole compound and context must be checked. |
| tomorrow | [ASLU TOMORROW](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/tomorrow.htm) | Single lexical TOMORROW versus repeated movement DAILY/EVERYDAY is explicit. Check cheek-area trajectory against reference performance and number of repetitions. Tomorrow is not an unconditional alias for future/later. |
| yesterday | [ASLU YESTERDAY](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/y/yesterday.htm) | A-hand recommended, Y-hand version also documented as used by Deaf signers. Preserve legitimate variant labels rather than declaring all initialization invalid. Verify handshape/location/movement from the linked performance; do not broaden to all past tense. |
| morning | [ASLU MORNING](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/m/morning.htm) | Palm-up dominant hand rises over base-arm horizon; wrist/base-finger contact. All/every/early morning and one-handed greeting realizations are separately described. Inspect contact and movement extent; bare MORNING is not a complete GOOD-MORNING phrase approval. |
| night | [ASLU NIGHT](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/n/night.htm) | Dominant wrist over horizontal base hand with fingers downward; evening context is documented. Sweep gives EVERY-NIGHT; TONIGHT is NOW+NIGHT; GOOD-NIGHT has compounds. Check hand overlap and variants; do not alias tonight/nightly to the isolated lexical clip. |
| money | [ASLU MONEY](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/m/money.htm) | Flattened O taps base palm twice. CASH and cashing a check require distinct meanings/context. Casual finger-rubbing gesture is explicitly not the formal lexical replacement. Check shape and contact; do not automatically alias cash/pay/deposit to MONEY. |
| buy | [ASLU BUY/PURCHASE](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/b/buy.htm) | Money-like dominant hand moves from base palm outward; repeated smaller motion is SHOPPING. Multiple O/flat-O realizations are linked. Check separation and repetition. Purchase is a candidate same sense; shopping, pay and metaphorical buy/believe need distinct review. |
| ask | [ASLU ASK](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/a/ask.htm) | Directional ASK-to changes index/X shape and direction with participants; other advanced variants exist. Formal non-directional REQUEST is different, with pray/plead readings dependent on movement and expression. Identify source realization; do not infer participants or add request/pray aliases blindly. |
| feel | [ASLU FEEL](https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/f/feel.htm) | Bent middle fingertip moves up chest, sometimes without contact. Repetition may convey feelings; a wh-question can rely on facial grammar. FEEL-LIKE is linked separately. Keep internal sensation/mood candidate sense distinct from touching an object, resemblance or desire. |

## Import and human-review decisions still required

1. Deduplicate source hashes and motion content before associating these glosses with installed records. Same English label is not proof of identical meaning; identical animation is not proof that conflicting source labels are interchangeable.
2. Record the actual sense and variant of each source performance. Candidate aliases in the table must retain pending mapping review until a qualified reviewer approves their use for that exact target animation.
3. Compare original performance and retargeted Aarav frames against the same ASL reference: handshape, palm orientation, location, trajectory, repetitions, two-hand contact/coordination, facial markers and body expression. A successful skeletal playback test cannot replace that comparison.
4. Require fluent-signer/interpreter review of the exact rendered target, date, identified reviewer and findings before default vocabulary eligibility. This audit supplies no such approval and changes no review flags.
5. Review sentence templates separately. Questions, negative clauses, participant direction, time/aspect, compounds and facial grammar cannot be approved by concatenating this list of lexical candidates.
